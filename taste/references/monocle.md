# Monocle

**Tags:** `editorial-print` `magazine` `briefing` `sections` `serif` `dense`

The magazine, first, and the site as the same briefing. Not a 2026 stylesheet, and not the shop.

## Description

A bound briefing. The launch issue, February 2007, ran long and was divided into named sections: Affairs, Business, Culture, Design, and Edits. Later years kept that spine and added to it (Fashion among them). Pages are dense: several short reports, then a longer story, photographs and illustration mixed with columns of text. The founding design notes name Plantin for the text and Helvetica Neue for captions and information, inside a strict grid. The print object is the point. The site is how a subscriber finds the same sections again.

## Key decisions

- The page is a contents, not a single essay and not a front-page ranking (`density.match-job`).
- Sections are named and stable. A reader can re-enter at Affairs or Design without a new navigation idea each visit (`density.repeat-rhythm`).
- A serif carries the report. A sans carries the caption, the folio, the data (`type.roles`, `type.two-families`).
- Illustration and photography are commissioned for the story. They are not stock decoration (`material.image-or-type`).
- Density is earned by real items. A briefing with nothing to brief is only cramped (`density.not-cramped`).

## Why it works

The job is orientation: several beats, one editorial voice, a way back into the section you trust. A cinematic still would answer a different question, and a newspaper lead would throw away the sections that are not today's top story. The grid lets a short report and a photograph share a page without becoming cards (`material.no-nested-cards`). You skim the sans labels and read the serif.

## What to steal

- A small set of named sections that match how the reader already thinks.
- A contents or a section opener that is type, a rule, and a list of real headlines.
- Serif prose, sans for the apparatus (caption, page label, nav).
- Mixed lengths: a few lines for a note, a real column for a story, with the same margins.
- Illustration used as information (a map, a portrait, a diagram), with a caption.

## What not to copy

- Plantin or Helvetica Neue unless you have licensed them. The 2007–2008 notes are a description of their pages, not a font assignment for yours (`type.license`).
- Their retail and café life as the layout. The briefing is the reference. A product grid is a different job.
- A black cover frame, a serif, and a travel photograph on the generic skeleton. That is the costume.
- Print density copied as 9px web type. Their page is paper. On screen, body stays readable (`a11y.size`).
- An invented palette. This file has no hex.

## Uniqueness

Cereal is one place, photographed, with hierarchy from space and very little apparatus. The Financial Times is a salmon daily that ranks and counts. The New York Times is a white front page. Monocle is the sectioned briefing: more items than Cereal, slower than a newspaper, and organized by a beat rather than by "what just happened."

## Product fit

A magazine, a seasonal report, a journal with stable departments, a briefing email that is actually edited.

A poor fit for a task list, a checkout, a single product, or a night listing. Do not use it when you have only one story.

## Source note
