# Criterion Collection

**Tags:** `cinematic-dark` `film` `library` `spine-number` `essay` `stills` `stable-frame`

The Collection's public graphic language: spines, editions, essays, and the Channel's collection art. Not a pixel audit of the current streaming chrome.

## Description

A library, not a storefront. A film is a spine number, a title, a still or a poster, a credit, and often an essay. The frame is black and white with the Criterion C. Color comes from the art of that film. Collection graphics made for the Channel are designed per program (a theme, a star, a period) in a 16:9 hero and further crops. The frame stays recognizable. The picture changes with the subject.

Essays are part of the product. A film page without a point of view would be a poster with a rent button. The number on the spine is information design: a stable id in a catalog that has opinions.

## Key decisions

- A repeating frame (mark, number, title, credit, still) and a varying interior (`density.repeat-rhythm`).
- The essay is a first-class object, set as reading, not as a "story" card under a hero.
- Artwork is specific to the work. A collection about one idea gets its own image, made for that idea, not a filtered keyart with a gradient scrim.
- Black is the shelf. It is not a luxury texture, and it is not gold-edged.
- The C and the number do more identity work than a type trend does.

## Why it works

You can scan a wall of spines because the frame is identical and the art is not (`uniqueness.one-idea`). The number gives the catalog a memory. The essay gives a choice a reason, which is a more premium form of "social proof" than a star row (`content.fake-proof` is the failure this avoids).

## What to steal

- A stable row or spine: identifier, title, one meta line, one image.
- An essay or a program note set in a reading column, with the still separate from the text.
- Per-item art direction inside a frame you do not restyle every time.
- Black and white as the shelf, so the film's own palette stays intact.
- An identifier a person can say out loud ("spine 584"), if your catalog has a real one.

## What not to copy

- Gold rules, a fake "EST." line, and letterspaced small caps on a SaaS hero. That is `color.gold-on-black` wearing a film costume.
- A black theme with no works in it.
- The C, the spine device, or their essay voice. Those are the brand. The lesson is "a catalog has a frame and a text."
- A carousel of posters with the titles over a scrim, which throws away the frame's alignment.
- Treating every customer as a curator. The point of view has to be the product's, in specific words.

## Uniqueness

MUBI's public site is a broadsheet with on/off rooms. RA is a schedule. Criterion is a library: numbers, credits, and writing. Use it when the screen is "choose one work from many, and understand why it is here."

## Product fit

Film, books, records, editions, archives, any catalog that can sustain an identifier and a short text.

A poor fit for a single product page (use quiet-luxury) and for operational data (use precise-technical). A table of invoices does not need a spine number unless the number is real.
